Why is a⁰ = 1?

It’s more than a rule to memorise. Let’s see why it makes sense—for any non-zero number.

Free lesson With Minidu Explains
a⁰ = ?

Start with the question.

Prefer YouTube? Open the video ↗

Read at your own pace

Follow the pattern.

Start with powers of 2. Every time the exponent decreases by one, divide the result by 2.

2³ 8
÷ 2 →
2² 4
÷ 2 →
2¹ 2
÷ 2 →
2⁰ 1

To continue that pattern, 2⁰ must equal 2 ÷ 2, which is 1. The same reasoning works with any non-zero base.

Now explain it using division.

We can calculate 5³ ÷ 5³ in two ways:

Divide the values 125 ÷ 125 = 1
Subtract the exponents 5³ ÷ 5³ = 5³⁻³ = 5⁰

Both expressions describe the same division, so 5⁰ = 1 .

The idea to keep

For a ≠ 0, aⁿ ÷ aⁿ = aⁿ⁻ⁿ = a⁰ = 1.

Dividing a non-zero number by itself gives 1.

Why do we exclude zero?

This argument would require dividing by zero, which is undefined. It therefore does not establish a value for 0⁰. The treatment of 0⁰ depends on the mathematical context.

Your turn

Check your understanding

Try each question before checking the explanation. These questions are free, too.

One last question

Could you explain it to a friend?

Without looking above, explain why 7⁰ = 1. Include the reason your argument doesn’t apply to a base of zero.

Compare with an explanation

7 ÷ 7 = 1. Using the exponent rule, 7¹ ÷ 7¹ = 7¹⁻¹ = 7⁰. So 7⁰ = 1. We cannot repeat this with zero because 0 ÷ 0 is undefined.
